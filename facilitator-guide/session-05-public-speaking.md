# Session 5 – Public speaking and presentations

**Duration:** 75 minutes  
**Primary outcome:** The participant can deliver a short, audience-focused presentation and respond to questions without becoming defensive or losing the main message.

## Preparation

Ask the participant to prepare a five-minute talk about a technical decision, project lesson, or engineering topic. It must not include confidential company information. Prepare a timer and a simple feedback rubric.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Check-in and homework review |
| 10–20 | Audience, purpose, and narrative |
| 20–30 | Presentation structure and delivery |
| 30–50 | Five-minute presentation and Q&A |
| 50–65 | Targeted practice and repeat |
| 65–75 | Reflection and homework |

## 1. Check-in (0–10)

Review the three versions of the participant's work update. Ask which audience was hardest to address and why.

## 2. Audience and purpose (10–20)

Before preparing a presentation, define:
- Audience: who is listening and what do they know?
- Purpose: inform, persuade, align, or request a decision?
- Takeaway: what should the audience remember?
- Action: what should happen after the presentation?

Ask: “If people remember only one sentence, what should it be?”

## 3. Presentation structure (20–30)

Teach a simple narrative:
1. **Context:** why this topic matters.
2. **Challenge:** what problem or opportunity exists.
3. **Insight:** what was learned or discovered.
4. **Recommendation:** what should happen.
5. **Ask:** what decision or action is needed.

Delivery guidance:
- Open with the point, not a long apology or biography.
- Use signposts: “There are two reasons…”, “The key trade-off is…”.
- Pause after important points.
- Keep slides supportive; do not read them aloud.
- If you do not know an answer, say so and explain how you will follow up.

## 4. Presentation and Q&A (30–50)

Participant delivers the prepared talk (up to five minutes). Then ask three questions:
- One clarification question.
- One challenge to an assumption.
- One question about trade-offs or consequences.

Observe:
- Clear opening and takeaway.
- Logical flow.
- Audience-appropriate detail.
- Pace, pauses, and audible delivery.
- Response to questions.
- Ability to acknowledge uncertainty.

Do not score accent, extroversion, or personality. Assess clarity and audience connection.

## 5. Targeted practice (50–65)

Choose one improvement area and rehearse:
- **If the opening is weak:** repeat the first 30 seconds with a clear takeaway.
- **If answers become defensive:** pause, paraphrase the question, answer the core concern.
- **If details overwhelm:** state the conclusion first, then ask whether more detail is useful.
- **If uncertainty is hidden:** practice “I don't have that data yet. I can validate it and follow up by [time].”

Repeat a 2–3 minute section.

## 6. Reflection and homework (65–75)

Ask:
- “What did the audience need most?”
- “Which question triggered the strongest reaction?”
- “What will you change next time?”

Homework:
- Deliver a five-minute explanation to a trusted colleague or small group.
- Ask for feedback on clarity, structure, and usefulness.
- Record a short practice version if comfortable; review only for the chosen skill.

## Facilitator watch-outs

- Do not confuse charisma with communication effectiveness.
- Avoid overwhelming the participant with many delivery corrections.
- Make Q&A challenging but fair; do not use hostile or humiliating questions.
- Respect accessibility needs and different communication styles.

## Completion criteria

The participant delivers a coherent presentation with a clear takeaway and responds to challenge by clarifying, reasoning, and acknowledging uncertainty appropriately.
