# Session 7 – Leadership communication and professional presence

**Duration:** 75 minutes  
**Primary outcome:** The participant can lead a focused technical discussion, create space for others, communicate ownership, and help a group reach a decision.

## Preparation

Prepare a 15-minute meeting scenario with a clear objective, 3–4 agenda items, and a decision to reach. Ask the participant to choose a real meeting they could facilitate before the next session.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Check-in and conflict homework |
| 10–22 | What leadership communication looks like |
| 22–32 | Meeting facilitation toolkit |
| 32–57 | Simulated meeting |
| 57–68 | Feedback and repeat |
| 68–75 | Homework and close |

## 1. Check-in (0–10)

Ask what happened in the real disagreement. Focus on the participant's behavior and the decision process, not whether they got their preferred outcome.

## 2. Leadership communication (10–22)

Leadership communication is not limited to a formal title. It includes:
- Making the goal and context clear.
- Inviting relevant perspectives.
- Making disagreement safe and productive.
- Naming uncertainty and trade-offs.
- Clarifying decisions and ownership.
- Following through.

Discuss the difference between contributing expertise and dominating the conversation. A strong technical contributor makes the group smarter, not merely more aware of their own expertise.

## 3. Meeting facilitation toolkit (22–32)

Use a simple meeting structure:

**Before**
- Define purpose: inform, discuss, decide, or plan.
- Invite only necessary participants.
- Share context and expected preparation.
- Identify the decision owner.

**During**
- Open with objective and desired outcome.
- Establish the agenda and time boundaries.
- Invite quieter voices without putting them on the spot.
- Summarize points of agreement and disagreement.
- Park unrelated topics.
- Confirm the decision and rationale.

**After**
- Publish decisions, owners, and due dates.
- Follow up on unresolved questions.
- Check whether the meeting achieved its purpose.

Useful facilitation phrases:
- “Let's hear one perspective we have not discussed yet.”
- “I hear two different concerns. Let me summarize them.”
- “Are we deciding now, or identifying what evidence is missing?”
- “Who owns the next step, and by when?”

## 4. Simulated meeting (32–57)

Participant facilitates a 15-minute technical discussion. Assign the facilitator and roleplay participants different perspectives:
- Engineer concerned about technical risk.
- Product stakeholder focused on timing.
- Teammate who speaks frequently.
- Teammate who has not yet contributed.

The facilitator observes whether the participant:
- States the objective.
- Manages time and scope.
- Invites relevant perspectives.
- Handles interruption respectfully.
- Summarizes trade-offs.
- Confirms decisions, owners, and next steps.

## 5. Feedback and repeat (57–68)

Ask the participant to assess themselves first. Then provide feedback on no more than two behaviors.

Repeat the meeting opening or the most difficult moment. Encourage the participant to use a concise summary and invite input.

## 6. Homework (68–75)

Lead one real 10–15 minute technical discussion or meeting segment. Beforehand, define the desired outcome. Afterward, ask two participants for brief feedback:
- Was the purpose clear?
- Did everyone have an appropriate opportunity to contribute?
- Was the decision or next step clear?

Do not request confidential feedback or attribute comments without consent.

## Facilitator watch-outs

- Do not imply that leadership requires being the loudest person.
- Do not reward meetings that end on time but fail to make progress.
- Avoid over-facilitating; the participant must practice ownership.
- Distinguish facilitation skill from formal decision authority.

## Completion criteria

The participant can lead a focused discussion, manage competing perspectives respectfully, and close with clear decisions or next steps.
