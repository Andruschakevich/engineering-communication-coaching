# Facilitator Guide

A practical delivery manual for coaches facilitating the eight-session Engineering Communication Coaching Program.

> **How to navigate:** Use the [main README](../README.md) for the complete coaching lifecycle. Use this folder when preparing to facilitate a particular session.

## Before facilitating

- For the pilot, review the [Participant-Driven Session Adaptations](../pilot-toolkit/participant-driven-session-adaptations.md) alongside the standard session guide. It maps participant-raised needs to the existing sessions and suggests tailored practice cases and a coaching/mentoring approach.

- Read the matching session in the [Curriculum](../curriculum/README.md).
- Read the relevant facilitator session guide below.
- Review the participant's agreed goals and previous commitments in the approved private coaching workspace.
- Choose one realistic practice scenario and adapt it to the participant's role and context.
- Open the matching assignment in the [Homework Library](../homework/README.md), then assign the Must do items and offer Nice to do extensions as appropriate.
- Keep participant ownership central: the coach facilitates; the participant chooses and applies behavior changes.

## Standard 75-minute session

| Stage | Time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Check-in | 10 min | Connect, review energy and recent situations |
| Homework review | 10 min | Notice attempts, outcomes, and learning |
| Model or concept | 15 min | Introduce one practical framework |
| Practice | 25 min | Rehearse realistic situations |
| Feedback and reflection | 10 min | Identify strengths and one adjustment |
| Commitment | 5 min | Agree on a small observable action |

If only 60 minutes are available, shorten the concept explanation and homework review; protect practice and reflection time.

## Facilitation loop

1. **Explain:** State the skill and why it matters at work.
2. **Demonstrate:** Model a weak version and an effective version.
3. **Practice:** Let the participant try before giving the answer.
4. **Observe:** Note exact words, behaviors, and their likely impact.
5. **Debrief:** Ask the participant to assess their own performance first.
6. **Repeat:** Rehearse the same moment with one adjustment.
7. **Transfer:** Choose a real, low-risk situation in which to use the skill.

## Feedback formula

Use **Situation – Behavior – Impact – Next step**:
- Situation: identify the specific moment.
- Behavior: describe what was said or done without labels.
- Impact: explain the effect or risk.
- Next step: suggest a concrete alternative and invite another attempt.

Example: “In the design discussion, when you said ‘this is a stupid approach,’ the conversation shifted from evaluating the design to defending the people involved. Try ‘I see a reliability risk in this approach; can we examine the failure mode?’ Let's rehearse that.”

## Psychological safety and accountability

- Be warm, direct, and specific. Do not soften important feedback until it becomes unclear.
- Do not shame, diagnose, or use sarcasm in response to sarcasm.
- Ask permission before exploring a sensitive example.
- Acknowledge intent while still discussing impact.
- If the participant rejects an observation, ask what evidence would help both of you understand the situation.
- Coaching does not replace management feedback, HR processes, or formal accountability.

## Session completion standard

A session is complete when the participant can:
- explain the skill in their own words;
- demonstrate it in at least one practice scenario;
- identify one strength and one improvement;
- name a realistic situation to apply it;
- commit to one observable action before the next session.

## Program-level review

Use the competency scorecard at baseline, around session 4, and after session 8. Rate behaviors against examples, not impressions. Invite participant self-assessment and, where appropriate and agreed, limited feedback from relevant colleagues or the manager. Avoid treating a numerical score as a diagnosis or as a standalone performance decision.
