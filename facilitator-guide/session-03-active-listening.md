# Session 3 – Active listening and constructive feedback

**Duration:** 75 minutes  
**Primary outcome:** The participant can listen to understand, check their interpretation, and give feedback that is specific and actionable.

## Preparation

Prepare a neutral work topic for listening practice and two short feedback scenarios. Review the participant's rewritten messages from Session 2.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Check-in and homework review |
| 10–22 | Listening behaviors |
| 22–35 | Feedback framework |
| 35–60 | Listening and feedback drills |
| 60–70 | Reflection and transfer |
| 70–75 | Homework |

## 1. Review (0–10)

Ask the participant to choose one rewritten message and explain what changed. Discuss whether clarity and directness were preserved.

## 2. Active listening (10–22)

Introduce the listening loop:
1. **Attend:** stop preparing your rebuttal; give the speaker space.
2. **Clarify:** ask open questions.
3. **Paraphrase:** summarize the meaning in your own words.
4. **Validate:** acknowledge the concern without necessarily agreeing.
5. **Confirm:** ask whether you understood correctly.
6. **Respond:** offer your view after understanding the other person's.

Useful questions:
- “What is the main concern for you?”
- “Can you give me an example?”
- “What outcome are you hoping for?”
- “What constraints am I missing?”
- “Have I understood correctly that…?”

Clarify that validation is not agreement. “I understand why the deadline worries you” does not mean “I agree with your proposed solution.”

## 3. Constructive feedback (22–35)

Teach **Situation – Behavior – Impact – Next step**.

Example:
“In yesterday's incident review (Situation), you interrupted the analyst twice before they finished explaining the timeline (Behavior). We missed some context and the discussion became tense (Impact). Next time, let them finish and then ask your questions (Next step).”

For positive feedback, be equally specific:
“In the release review, you summarized the risk and asked for the QA view before recommending a decision. That helped the group make a clear call.”

Teach feedback principles:
- Timely, private when corrective, and proportionate.
- Based on observable behavior, not assumptions.
- Focused on a change within the person's control.
- Open to the recipient's perspective.

## 4. Practice drills (35–60)

**Drill A – Listen without fixing (8 minutes)**  
The facilitator describes a work challenge for two minutes. The participant may only ask clarifying questions and paraphrase. No advice until invited.

**Drill B – Confirm the concern (8 minutes)**  
Scenario: A developer says QA is slowing delivery. The participant must uncover the underlying concern, constraints, and desired outcome before responding.

**Drill C – Give corrective feedback (9 minutes)**  
Scenario: A teammate repeatedly sends incomplete handoffs. The participant gives feedback using the four-part framework and checks for the other person's view.

After each drill, ask the participant to self-assess first. Repeat one moment with a single adjustment.

## 5. Reflection (60–70)

Ask:
- “When did you most want to interrupt?”
- “What did paraphrasing reveal?”
- “Did you separate understanding from agreement?”
- “Was the next step clear and within the person's control?”

## 6. Homework (70–75)

In three real conversations:
- Ask at least two clarifying questions before offering your view.
- Paraphrase the other person's main concern.
- Confirm whether your summary is accurate.

Also give one piece of specific positive feedback and one appropriate piece of developmental feedback. Reflect on the outcome without recording private conversation details.

## Facilitator watch-outs

- Do not let paraphrasing become robotic repetition.
- Avoid “feedback sandwiches” that obscure the actual message.
- Do not assume the speaker's emotion or motive; ask.
- If a conversation is too sensitive or high-stakes, practice with a fictional scenario.

## Completion criteria

The participant can demonstrate the listening loop and deliver feedback that identifies a situation, observable behavior, impact, and actionable next step.
