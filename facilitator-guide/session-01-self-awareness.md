# Session 1 – Self-awareness and communication assessment

**Duration:** 75 minutes  
**Primary outcome:** The participant understands their current communication patterns, identifies situations that are difficult for them, and selects one behavior to experiment with.

## Preparation

- Review the competency model and initial assessment template.
- Prepare a blank communication diary.
- Ask the participant to bring one recent conversation that went well and one that felt difficult. They may summarize or sanitize the examples.
- Explain confidentiality, record-keeping, and what high-level progress information may be shared with a manager.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Welcome, purpose, working agreement |
| 10–20 | Participant's goals and strengths |
| 20–35 | Communication competency self-assessment |
| 35–55 | Situation mapping and pattern exploration |
| 55–68 | Select a development focus and practice |
| 68–75 | Commitments and homework |

## 1. Opening and agreement (0–10)

**Suggested opening:**  
“This is a development conversation, not a disciplinary meeting. The goal is to make your communication as effective as your technical contribution. We will work with concrete situations, practice alternatives, and review what helps. I will be candid and respectful; I expect you to be open to experimenting.”

Clarify:
- What the coaching is and is not.
- What notes will be kept and where.
- What may be shared with the manager, and what remains private.
- That the participant can ask to pause or choose a different example.

**Ask:** “What would make these sessions genuinely useful to you?”

## 2. Strengths and goals (10–20)

Ask:
- “When does communication work especially well for you?”
- “What do teammates rely on you for?”
- “What kind of conversations drain your energy?”
- “If colleagues noticed one positive change in two months, what would you want it to be?”

Listen without correcting the participant's account too early. Reflect strengths explicitly.

## 3. Baseline self-assessment (20–35)

Use the competency scorecard. Ask the participant to rate themselves and provide a recent example for each relevant area:
- Respectful communication
- Emotional awareness
- Active listening
- Constructive feedback
- Structured communication
- Conflict resolution
- Presentation and facilitation
- Collaboration and professional presence

Use a 1–5 behavioral scale. Do not average scores into a single label. Note confidence and evidence separately.

**Probe:** “What would a colleague see or hear that makes this a 3 rather than a 2?”

## 4. Situation mapping (35–55)

Draw a simple sequence:

**Trigger → Interpretation → Emotion/urge → Words/action → Impact → Alternative**

Choose one difficult example and explore:
- What happened immediately before the exchange?
- What did you assume the other person meant?
- What did you want to achieve?
- What did you actually say or do?
- How might the other person have experienced it?
- What evidence supports that interpretation? What else could be true?

Avoid diagnosing personality or assigning motives. Separate observable facts from interpretations.

## 5. First rehearsal (55–68)

Ask the participant to replay the situation using a more effective response.

Example:
- First attempt: “This makes no sense. Did you even think about the edge cases?”
- Alternative: “I’m concerned we have not covered the failure cases. Can we walk through timeout and retry behavior before deciding?”

Give feedback using Situation–Behavior–Impact–Next step. Ask the participant to repeat the response once.

## 6. Close and homework (68–75)

Agree on one focus behavior, such as pausing before responding when frustrated.

Assign the [Personal AI Communication Coach mini-project](../homework/01-ai-communication-companion.md) as the main between-session assignment:
- create a reusable Communication Coach Skill;
- configure a morning preparation task and an evening reflection task;
- test both for at least three working days;
- bring a sanitized summary of what worked, what was inaccurate, and what was improved.

The [Communication Diary](../homework/02-communication-diary.md) can be used as a supporting practice. Keep it lightweight and do not duplicate the same observations in multiple places.

**Privacy gate:** The participant must confirm company policy and approved tooling before any AI processing of work communications. The agent must use only explicitly authorized sources and the minimum necessary scope. If AI processing is not permitted, use participant-written summaries or sanitized examples and document the manual alternative. Never ask for raw chat exports, screenshots, or confidential messages.

## Facilitator watch-outs

- Do not turn the first session into a list of complaints from others.
- Do not argue with the participant's self-rating; ask for examples.
- Do not set too many goals. One behavior is enough.
- If the participant feels ambushed, revisit the purpose and agreement before proceeding.
- Do not treat an AI agent's output as an objective assessment or allow it to make decisions for the participant.

## Completion criteria

The participant can name at least two communication strengths, identify one recurring challenging context, explain one behavior-impact connection, and commit to a specific experiment.
