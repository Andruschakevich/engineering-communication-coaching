# Session 8 – Review and development plan

**Duration:** 75 minutes  
**Primary outcome:** The participant can recognize progress, identify remaining development areas, and commit to a realistic 90-day plan with observable behaviors and feedback loops.

## Preparation

- Review the baseline assessment and midpoint notes.
- Ask the participant to bring examples of attempts, successes, and setbacks from the program.
- Prepare the competency scorecard and 90-day development plan template.
- Confirm what information may be shared with the manager under the original agreement.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Check-in and participant reflection |
| 10–25 | Review evidence and outcomes |
| 25–40 | Endline competency assessment |
| 40–58 | Build the 90-day plan |
| 58–68 | Accountability and support |
| 68–75 | Close and next steps |

## 1. Participant reflection (0–10)

Ask:
- “What are you most proud of?”
- “Which behavior changed most?”
- “What remains difficult?”
- “What feedback surprised you?”
Let the participant speak before presenting your observations.

## 2. Review evidence (10–25)

Review examples from across the program:
- Respectful disagreement.
- A moment of active listening.
- A piece of constructive feedback.
- A structured technical explanation.
- A presentation or Q&A.
- A conflict or facilitated discussion.

For each example, distinguish:
- What the participant tried.
- What was observable.
- What impact or outcome followed.
- What remains uncertain.

Look for patterns, not isolated perfection. A setback does not erase progress; repeated patterns still need attention.

## 3. Endline assessment (25–40)

Repeat the competency scorecard using the same 1–5 behavioral anchors as baseline. Ask for evidence for each rating.

Compare baseline and endline carefully:
- Where is there clear behavioral evidence of progress?
- Where is the evidence mixed?
- Which areas need more practice?
- Does the participant's self-assessment differ from observed examples?

Do not promise or imply a guaranteed score increase. The purpose is a useful development conversation, not a performance verdict.

## 4. Build the 90-day plan (40–58)

Limit the plan to 2–3 priorities. For each priority define:
- Target behavior.
- Why it matters.
- Specific actions and situations to practice.
- Evidence of progress.
- Feedback source or method.
- Review date.

Example:

**Priority:** Respond constructively to disagreement.  
**Behavior:** Ask one clarifying question before challenging a proposal; criticize the idea, not the person.  
**Practice:** Use this in two design discussions per month.  
**Evidence:** Short reflection after each discussion and feedback from a trusted colleague.  
**Review:** At 30, 60, and 90 days.

Ensure goals are within the participant's influence and fit their actual role.

## 5. Accountability and support (58–68)

Ask:
- “What might get in the way?”
- “What will you do when you notice the old pattern returning?”
- “Who can give you useful, respectful feedback?”
- “What support do you need from your manager?”
- “How will you know the plan is becoming a habit?”

Agree whether a 30-day follow-up would be useful. Clarify who owns each action.

## 6. Close (68–75)

Recognize specific effort and progress. Invite the participant to state:
- One behavior they will continue.
- One behavior they will stop.
- One behavior they will start.
- One commitment for the next seven days.

Explain any agreed high-level summary that will be shared with the manager. Do not disclose private session notes outside the agreed boundaries.

## Facilitator watch-outs

- Avoid turning the final session into a pass/fail judgment.
- Do not overstate impact based on a small number of examples.
- Do not create a long wish list. Prioritize.
- Keep manager support and participant ownership distinct.

## Completion criteria

The participant has reviewed evidence, completed an endline self-assessment, selected 2–3 priorities, and created a practical 90-day plan with actions and review points.
