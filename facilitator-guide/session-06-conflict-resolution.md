# Session 6 – Conflict resolution and influencing

**Duration:** 75 minutes  
**Primary outcome:** The participant can de-escalate disagreement, uncover interests, compare options, and influence a decision without relying on status, sarcasm, or pressure.

## Preparation

Prepare a realistic cross-functional disagreement. Ideally use a participant-selected example with sensitive details removed. Identify the decision, stakeholders, constraints, and unresolved assumptions.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Check-in and presentation practice review |
| 10–22 | Conflict patterns and de-escalation |
| 22–35 | Shared-interest and decision framework |
| 35–60 | Roleplay with increasing difficulty |
| 60–70 | Debrief and transfer |
| 70–75 | Homework |

## 1. Check-in (0–10)

Ask what feedback the participant received after the short presentation. What did they learn about their response to questions?

## 2. Conflict patterns (10–22)

Discuss common unhelpful patterns:
- Attack: “You always break the process.”
- Defend: “This is not my fault.”
- Withdraw: avoid the issue.
- Escalate: involve authority before attempting clarification.
- Compete: focus on winning rather than solving.

Introduce a pause-and-reset:
1. Notice the trigger.
2. Slow the pace.
3. State the shared goal.
4. Ask what matters most to the other person.
5. Return to evidence and options.

Useful phrases:
- “I think we agree on the outcome but differ on the trade-off.”
- “What risk are you most concerned about?”
- “What evidence would change your view?”
- “Can we compare the options against the same criteria?”

## 3. Interests and decision framework (22–35)

Separate **positions** from **interests**:
- Position: “We must release today.”
- Possible interest: customer commitment, deadline, or avoiding rework.

Use this sequence:
1. Define the decision.
2. Identify shared outcome.
3. Surface each stakeholder's interests and constraints.
4. Separate facts from assumptions.
5. Generate options.
6. Compare options using agreed criteria.
7. Agree on owner, action, and review point.

Influence through clarity, evidence, empathy, and reliability—not manipulation.

## 4. Roleplay (35–60)

**Scenario – Release disagreement**  
A product stakeholder wants to release today. An engineer believes a failure mode has not been tested. The participant must reach a decision or define what evidence is needed.

Run three rounds:
- Round 1: cooperative counterpart.
- Round 2: counterpart challenges the participant's credibility or urgency.
- Round 3: counterpart interrupts and repeats their preferred solution.

The facilitator should increase pressure gradually, never insult or humiliate the participant.

Observe:
- Does the participant clarify interests?
- Do they acknowledge legitimate constraints?
- Do they distinguish evidence from assumptions?
- Do they offer options and trade-offs?
- Do they close with a decision, owner, and next step?

## 5. Debrief (60–70)

Ask:
- “What did the other person care about most?”
- “When did you shift from solving the problem to defending your position?”
- “What phrase helped lower tension?”
- “Was the final agreement explicit?”

Repeat one difficult moment with a different opening.

## 6. Homework (70–75)

Facilitate or participate in one real, low-risk disagreement. Before the conversation, write down:
- Decision to be made.
- Shared goal.
- Each party's likely interests (as hypotheses, not facts).
- Evidence and options.

Afterward, reflect on whether the discussion ended with a clear decision, owner, and next step.

## Facilitator watch-outs

- Do not frame every disagreement as a communication failure; sometimes the conflict is about real resource or priority constraints.
- Do not encourage false compromise when evidence supports a clear decision.
- Avoid coaching the participant to “win” or manipulate.
- If there is harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or a serious policy concern, use established company channels rather than treating it as a coaching exercise.

## Completion criteria

The participant can de-escalate a disagreement, identify interests, compare options against criteria, and close with a clear next step.
