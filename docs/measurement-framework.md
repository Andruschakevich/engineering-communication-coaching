# Measurement Framework

## Philosophy
Measure behavior change and practical effect using a small set of observable indicators and multiple perspectives. Do not imply that coaching alone caused every change.

## Review points
- Baseline: before session 1.
- Pulse: around session 4.
- Endline: after session 8.
- Sustainability: 30–90 days later.

## Evidence sources
Participant self-assessment, coach observations in practice, manager observations, and optional colleague feedback gathered transparently and with appropriate consent. Avoid broad 360-degree collection by default.

## Example indicators
- Fewer substantiated instances of disrespectful or personally directed communication.
- Lets others finish, clarifies, and paraphrases in observed discussions.
- Receives corrective feedback by asking for examples and agreeing on a next step.
- Explains a technical issue concisely for the intended audience.
- Facilitates a discussion that ends with a decision, owner, and next step.
- Completes agreed practice assignments and reflects honestly.

Write goals as: "During [situation], I will [observable behavior], measured by [evidence], reviewed on [date]."

Manager updates should normally cover attendance, agreed goals, high-level progress, commitments, and support needed—not private reflections, raw notes, or message transcripts unless explicitly agreed and appropriate.

A higher rating is not proof of lasting change. Look for repeated behavior across contexts and time. Discuss conflicting examples rather than forcing agreement.
