# Confidentiality, Privacy, and Ethics

Explain before collecting information what is collected, why, who can access it, what is shared with managers, retention expectations, and limits required by law or company policy. Follow applicable law and organizational privacy, security, HR, and records policies; this document is not legal advice.

## Data minimization
- Collect only examples needed for agreed goals.
- Prefer participant-selected examples and short contextual excerpts.
- Never request a full Slack, Teams, email, or chat export.
- Do not copy customer, security, personal, or proprietary information into this repository or external tools.
- Redact names and unrelated details.
- Store participant-specific notes separately in approved, access-controlled storage.

## Reviewing messages
Confirm the example may appropriately be shared and reviewed. Include enough context to interpret it fairly; analyze wording, audience, timing, and impact—not personality. Include effective examples as well as improvement opportunities. Do not publish raw messages or screenshots in this reusable repository. A pilot can begin with 5–10 selected examples rather than an indiscriminate archive review.

## Boundaries
Coaching is not a workplace investigation, performance-management substitute, therapy, or a way to conceal serious policy violations. Follow established processes for serious safety, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or other policy concerns. Explain confidentiality limits before starting.

## Manager alignment
Agree that the coach may report participation, progress against agreed goals, and support needed—but not private session details by default. The manager continues normal feedback and accountability.

Consider culture, language proficiency, neurodiversity, disability, power dynamics, and different communication norms. Do not equate extroversion, accent, or presentation style with competence.
