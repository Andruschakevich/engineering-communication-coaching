# Engineering Communication Coaching

A practical, reusable coaching program that helps software engineers strengthen communication, collaboration, emotional intelligence, presentations, conflict resolution, and professional influence.

> **For coaches:** Start here. This README is the entry point to prepare, launch, deliver, and complete a coaching engagement. Follow the stages in order; each stage links to the guide or template you need.

**The coaching journey:** [Prepare](#1-prepare-yourself) → [Align](#2-align-with-the-manager-or-sponsor) → [Kick off](#3-run-the-kick-off) → [Assess](#4-establish-the-baseline) → [Coach](#5-deliver-the-eight-sessions) → [Review](#6-review-progress-and-close) → [Sustain](#7-support-lasting-change)

## Quick start: what to do first

If you are a new coach, start with the one-page [Coach Onboarding Checklist](COACH_ONBOARDING_CHECKLIST.md). It confirms that the engagement, sponsor alignment, privacy boundaries, kick-off, and delivery materials are ready before you begin.

If this is your first time using the framework, follow these steps:

1. **Understand the approach:** read [Coaching Principles](COACHING_PRINCIPLES.md), [Program Overview](PROGRAM_OVERVIEW.md), and [Confidentiality & Ethics](docs/confidentiality-and-ethics.md).
2. **Understand the person and context:** clarify the development need, confirm that participation is voluntary, and identify the coach, participant, and manager/sponsor roles.
3. **Prepare the engagement:** use the [Coach's End-to-End Framework](docs/coaching-framework.md) and [Manager Alignment Guide](pilot-toolkit/manager-alignment.md).
4. **Run the kick-off:** open the [Kick-off Presentation & Checklist](pilot-toolkit/kickoff-presentation.md) and facilitate the [Initial Meeting Script](pilot-toolkit/initial-meeting-script.md).
5. **Set the baseline:** use the [Baseline Assessment](pilot-toolkit/baseline-assessment.md), [Competency Model](docs/competency-model.md), and [Initial Assessment Template](templates/initial-assessment.md).
6. **Agree on goals:** capture 2–3 observable, participant-owned goals and decide how progress will be reviewed.
7. **Deliver the sessions:** use the [Curriculum](curriculum/README.md), the [Facilitator Guide](facilitator-guide/README.md), and the session-by-session [Homework Library](homework/README.md).
8. **Review and sustain:** complete the [90-Day Development Plan](templates/90-day-development-plan.md) and the final review.

You do not need to read every file before starting. Use the stage-by-stage guide below and open the linked material when you reach that stage.

## The end-to-end coaching journey

### 1. Prepare yourself

**Goal:** Understand the framework and prepare to facilitate a safe, focused development process.

- [ ] Read the [Coaching Principles](COACHING_PRINCIPLES.md).
- [ ] Review the [Program Overview](PROGRAM_OVERVIEW.md).
- [ ] Understand the [Coaching Framework](docs/coaching-framework.md).
- [ ] Read [Confidentiality & Ethics](docs/confidentiality-and-ethics.md).
- [ ] Review the [Competency Model](docs/competency-model.md) and [Measurement Framework](docs/measurement-framework.md).
- [ ] Adapt examples and language to the participant's role, seniority, culture, and goals.

**Open:** [Coach's framework](docs/coaching-framework.md) · [Competency model](docs/competency-model.md) · [Ethics and confidentiality](docs/confidentiality-and-ethics.md)

### 2. Align with the manager or sponsor

**Goal:** Agree on the purpose and operating boundaries before inviting the participant into the process.

- [ ] Clarify the development need and desired outcomes.
- [ ] Confirm the coach, participant, and manager/sponsor roles.
- [ ] Make clear that coaching is developmental, not a covert performance review or investigation.
- [ ] Agree what high-level progress information may be shared and what remains private.
- [ ] Confirm time commitment, meeting cadence, and support the participant may need.
- [ ] Confirm any company policies that affect recording, transcription, AI tools, or workplace data.

**Open:** [Manager Alignment Guide](pilot-toolkit/manager-alignment.md)

### 3. Run the kick-off

**Goal:** Establish trust, explain the program, hear the participant's expectations, and agree how you will work together.

Use the presentation on a shared screen and move through its checklist in order.

- [ ] Open the [Kick-off Presentation & Checklist](pilot-toolkit/kickoff-presentation.md).
- [ ] Start with recording and transcription: explain purpose, tool/provider, processing, storage, access, retention, and alternatives.
- [ ] Obtain explicit consent from every participant before recording. If anyone declines, continue without recording.
- [ ] Explain the program, roles, expectations, and confidentiality boundaries.
- [ ] Ask about the participant's goals, concerns, preferences, and feedback on the proposed approach.
- [ ] Agree the dates and times for the sessions.
- [ ] Agree on communication channels and how to handle rescheduling.
- [ ] Confirm the first practical next steps and homework.

**Open:** [Kick-off Presentation](pilot-toolkit/kickoff-presentation.md) · [Initial Meeting Script](pilot-toolkit/initial-meeting-script.md) · [Coaching Agreement](pilot-toolkit/coaching-agreement.md)

### 4. Establish the baseline

**Goal:** Build a balanced starting picture using observable behaviors and multiple perspectives.

- [ ] Ask the participant to complete their own self-assessment.
- [ ] Invite the manager to provide observations based on normal work interactions, where appropriate and agreed.
- [ ] If permitted by company policy, the participant may use the [AI-assisted Communication Review Prompt](pilot-toolkit/ai-communication-review-prompt.md) to review their own work communications with an approved tool.
- [ ] Do not collect or share raw private messages, broad chat exports, customer data, or confidential workplace information.
- [ ] Review the AI-generated summary with the participant; treat it as an input with limitations, not an objective verdict.
- [ ] Compare perspectives through examples and discussion. Do not average scores into a single rating.
- [ ] Agree on 2–3 development priorities and define what observable progress would look like.

**Open:** [Baseline Assessment](pilot-toolkit/baseline-assessment.md) · [AI-assisted Review Prompt](pilot-toolkit/ai-communication-review-prompt.md) · [Competency Scorecard](templates/competency-scorecard.md) · [Initial Assessment](templates/initial-assessment.md)

### 5. Deliver the eight sessions

**Goal:** Turn development goals into repeatable behaviors through explanation, demonstration, practice, feedback, and real-world experiments.

The standard program has eight sessions. Adapt the sequence and pace to the participant; the curriculum is a reference path, not a rigid prescription.

| # | Session | Main outcome | Session guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Self-awareness & communication assessment | Recognize communication patterns, strengths, and priorities | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-01-self-awareness.md) |
| 2 | Respectful communication & emotional intelligence | Communicate directly and respectfully, including under pressure | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-02-respectful-communication.md) |
| 3 | Active listening & constructive feedback | Listen to understand and exchange feedback productively | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-03-active-listening.md) |
| 4 | Structured communication | Explain ideas clearly, concisely, and for the audience | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-04-structured-communication.md) |
| 5 | Public speaking & presentations | Present ideas and handle questions with confidence | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-05-public-speaking.md) |
| 6 | Conflict resolution & influencing | Navigate disagreement and reach constructive decisions | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-06-conflict-resolution.md) |
| 7 | Leadership communication & professional presence | Facilitate collaboration and influence without authority | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-07-leadership-communication.md) |
| 8 | Review & 90-day development plan | Review evidence and create a sustainable next-step plan | [Guide](facilitator-guide/session-08-review-and-development-plan.md) |

**For every session:**
- [ ] Check in and review a recent situation.
- [ ] Discuss the previous experiment or homework.
- [ ] Introduce one focused model or concept.
- [ ] Demonstrate an effective approach.
- [ ] Practice through a realistic scenario.
- [ ] Ask the participant to reflect first; then give specific feedback.
- [ ] Agree on one small, observable experiment before the next meeting.
- [ ] Record only the minimum necessary notes in an approved private location.

Use the [Weekly Progress Tracker](pilot-toolkit/weekly-progress-tracker.md) and [Session Agenda Template](templates/session-agenda.md).

### 6. Review progress and close

**Goal:** Evaluate change using evidence, reflect on the coaching process, and agree on what happens next.

- [ ] Revisit the original goals and baseline.
- [ ] Repeat relevant self-assessment and other agreed perspectives.
- [ ] Compare examples from the beginning and end of the program.
- [ ] Ask the participant what was useful, difficult, or should change.
- [ ] Identify skills that improved and areas that still need practice.
- [ ] Agree on a realistic 90-day continuation plan.
- [ ] Share only the progress summary agreed with the participant and manager/sponsor.

**Open:** [90-Day Development Plan](templates/90-day-development-plan.md) · [Personal Communication Playbook](templates/personal-communication-playbook.md) · [Weekly Program Review](pilot-toolkit/weekly-program-review.md)

### 7. Support lasting change

**Goal:** Help the participant continue practicing after the formal sessions end.

- [ ] Agree on a small number of recurring behaviors to maintain.
- [ ] Identify real work situations where the participant can practice.
- [ ] Schedule optional 30- or 90-day follow-up conversations.
- [ ] Review progress against observable examples, not memory or impressions alone.
- [ ] Capture only anonymized, generalizable lessons when improving this framework.

## How a typical session works

Recommended duration: **60–75 minutes**. For a 60-minute session, shorten the concept explanation and homework review while protecting practice and reflection.

| Stage | 60–75 min guide | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Check-in | 5–10 min | Connect and identify a relevant situation |
| Homework review | 5–10 min | Explore attempts, outcomes, and learning |
| Concept | 10–15 min | Introduce one practical model |
| Practice | 20–25 min | Rehearse a realistic situation |
| Feedback and reflection | 10 min | Identify strengths and one adjustment |
| Commitment | 5 min | Agree on one next action |

The core facilitation loop is **Explain → Demonstrate → Practice → Observe → Debrief → Repeat → Transfer**. See the [Facilitator Guide](facilitator-guide/README.md) for detailed guidance.

## Roles and terminology

- **Coach:** facilitates the process, provides structure and feedback, and supports practice.
- **Participant / mentee:** owns their goals, reflections, decisions, and behavior experiments.
- **Manager / sponsor:** supports the development opportunity and provides agreed high-level observations; does not receive private coaching notes by default.

Use **participant** as the default neutral term throughout the materials. **Mentee** is also acceptable where it fits the relationship.

## Measurement principles

- Focus on observable behaviors, not personality labels or assumptions about intent.
- Use self-assessment, manager observations, and AI-assisted review only where appropriate and permitted. Coach observations may inform qualitative feedback; they are not a substitute for participant ownership.
- Treat AI-assisted assessment as a fallible input, not an authoritative score.
- Record evidence confidence and use **N/O (not observed)** when evidence is insufficient.
- Do not calculate one combined score or use coaching ratings as a standalone performance decision.
- Review progress against the participant's agreed goals and real examples.

## Privacy, recording, and responsible AI use

- Participation should be voluntary and informed.
- Obtain explicit consent from every participant before recording a meeting. If anyone declines, continue without recording.
- Explain transcription and any AI/third-party processing separately; recording consent does not automatically authorize external AI processing.
- Confirm company policy and applicable requirements before using workplace communications with any AI tool. A coding assistant is not automatically local-only.
- Never place raw work messages, chat exports, recordings, transcripts, customer data, or identifiable coaching notes in this reusable repository.
- Keep participant-specific materials in an approved, access-controlled location. Share only the summaries and progress information agreed in advance.
- See [Confidentiality & Ethics](docs/confidentiality-and-ethics.md).

## Scope and limitations

This is a developmental coaching framework. It is not therapy, an investigation, an HR process, a disciplinary procedure, or a replacement for performance management. Serious policy or conduct concerns must be handled through the appropriate organizational channels.

## Contributing to the framework

Improve the reusable materials through anonymized, generalizable learning. Do not add participant names, private messages, identifiable incidents, or personal assessment records. Update relevant folder indexes when adding or moving documents.
