# Homework — Difficult Communication Reflection

**Purpose:** Build the habit of responding constructively when communication is unclear, incomplete, or difficult.
**Practice period:** One working week.
**Time:** 10–15 minutes at the end of each working day.

## Daily practice

At the end of each working day, choose one naturally occurring work interaction related to the topic. Examples include:

- Someone did not reply.
- Someone replied briefly, dryly, or ambiguously.
- A request or feedback was vague.
- Someone interrupted or strongly disagreed.
- Another communication situation made it difficult to understand the message or move the work forward.

Do not create a difficult interaction just to complete the exercise. If no relevant example occurred, note that and move on.

For the selected example, write a short, sanitized reflection:

1. **What happened?** Record observable facts; do not infer the other person's intent.
2. **What did I think or feel?** Separate your reaction from what is known.
3. **What did I need to clarify or obtain?** For example, an answer, criterion, decision, deadline, or next step.
4. **How did I respond, or how could I respond?** Consider clarifying, asking for specifics, following up with a clear timeframe, setting a boundary, or changing the channel.
5. **What happened next?** Did the exchange produce clarity, an agreement, or a next step?
6. **What would I try in a similar situation next time?** Name one specific behavior or phrase.

Keep the entry brief. Do not copy messages, include names, share screenshots, or record confidential work details. Use anonymized notes and follow workplace data policies.

## Review at the next coaching session

Choose a few entries and discuss:

- Where did you separate facts from assumptions?
- Which questions or responses helped clarify the situation?
- What next step worked?
- What felt difficult, and what would you like to try next?

## Completion standard

The participant can describe at least one real example, identify a specific response choice, and reflect on its observed effect. The exercise is for learning, not for judging the participant or the other person.
