# Homework — Session 2 Follow-Through

**Purpose:** Continue reflective use of an AI communication coach, practice responses to difficult communication in real work situations, take a step toward public-speaking practice, and clarify communication expectations for a target role.
**Timing:** Agree a realistic review period and manager-meeting deadline with the participant. Add the recurring reflection blocks and planned meetings to the participant's calendar.

## 1. Continue the morning AI report reflection

Continue reviewing the agreed AI communication-coach report in the morning. Add a short calendar reminder next to the report review. Reflect on:

- What was useful?
- Which recommendation did you apply or choose not to apply, and why?
- Did the report help you prepare for meetings?
- Where did it miss context or make an unsupported inference?
- Was the amount and format useful?

Use only tools, data sources, and work information permitted by workplace policy. Do not provide raw, identifiable, or confidential correspondence.

## 2. Add an end-of-day communication reflection

At the end of each working day, schedule **10–15 minutes** to reflect on one naturally occurring work interaction related to difficult or unclear communication. For example, someone did not respond, replied briefly or ambiguously, gave vague feedback, interrupted, or strongly disagreed.

Use the daily reflection prompts in [Difficult Communication Reflection](10-difficult-communication-reflection.md). If no relevant example occurred, note that and move on; do not create an interaction for the exercise.

## 3. Plan a Toastmasters visit

Choose a suitable Toastmasters club, check its upcoming schedule, contact the organizers or register, and add the planned meeting to your calendar. Attend a first meeting if the schedule allows.

## 4. Discuss expectations for a target role with your manager

Arrange and hold a one-to-one with your manager within the timeframe agreed in coaching. Clarify:

- The target role and its expected scope, outcomes, and behaviors.
- What communication at that level looks like: relevant audiences, topics, decision-making, trade-offs, risks, and cross-team or organizational impact.
- What observable examples would demonstrate readiness and how to receive specific developmental feedback.
- Who makes the promotion decision and who may influence it or provide relevant feedback.

Create a private stakeholder map. As a starting point, identify at least **3 decision makers** and **5 influencers**, adapting the scope if the organization or decision process is smaller. For each person, note their role, what they currently know about your contribution, what evidence or outcomes may be relevant, an appropriate work context/channel, one natural first step, and a question for useful feedback. Mark overlapping roles clearly.

Use the manager conversation to check your assumptions about target-level communication. Select one or two natural first steps to make relevant work and impact easier to understand. Do not ask people for a predetermined positive endorsement; seek accurate expectations and specific feedback. The goal is informed, evidence-based visibility, not control over a promotion outcome.

Keep names, individual feedback, and internal organization details in an approved private workspace. Do not add personal stakeholder maps or identifiable examples to this course repository.

## Review in the next coaching session

Bring sanitized learning, not raw messages or private notes:

- What did the AI report help you notice, and where was it wrong?
- What patterns did you notice in end-of-day reflections?
- Which club did you choose, and what is the next step?
- What did you learn about the target role and communication expectations?
- What first communication step will you try, and what feedback will help you evaluate it?

## Completion standard

The participant has calendarized the agreed reflection times, selected and scheduled a Toastmasters next step, discussed target-role expectations with the manager, and identified one realistic communication experiment. The exercise does not require a promotion outcome or positive feedback.
