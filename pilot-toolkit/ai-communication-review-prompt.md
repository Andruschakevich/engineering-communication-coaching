# AI-Assisted Work Communication Review — Prompt & Procedure

Use this workflow only after confirming that company policy permits the selected work-communication data to be processed by the selected AI provider, account, model, and tool. Claude Code is not automatically local-only; prompts and selected context may be transmitted to the provider.

## Pre-flight checks

Do not proceed unless all are true:
- You are authorized to access and process every included source.
- The company permits this purpose and this AI provider/tool.
- The account, model, privacy settings, retention, and data-handling configuration meet company requirements.
- Personal conversations and restricted data can be excluded.
- You will share only a sanitized summary—not raw messages, exports, screenshots, or verbatim excerpts.

Exclude personal conversations, credentials, customer secrets, sensitive personal data, HR/medical/legal matters, and any channel or conversation you are not authorized to process. Do not bypass access controls. If the data cannot be safely scoped, stop and ask for an approved alternative.

## Suggested Claude Code instruction

You are assisting me with a private, developmental self-review of my workplace communication. Analyze only the files and sources I explicitly identify as approved for this review. Do not search outside the approved input directory or access other accounts, repositories, channels, or systems.

The review period is: [START DATE] to [END DATE].

### Competencies

Assess observable communication behaviors in these areas:

1. Respectful wording and professional tone
2. Respectful disagreement without personalization
3. Emotional self-awareness and self-regulation (only where observable; do not diagnose)
4. Active listening and clarification (only where the written record supports an inference)
5. Constructive feedback: specificity, evidence, impact, actionable next steps
6. Openness to feedback, accountability, and repair
7. Clarity, logical structure, audience adaptation, and conciseness
8. Conflict de-escalation and evidence-based persuasion
9. Decision, owner, and action clarity
10. Collaboration, reliability, and follow-through
11. Presentation/facilitation only if the approved written data contains relevant evidence; otherwise mark N/O.

### Analysis rules

- Review the approved corpus for recurring patterns, not isolated wording alone.
- Consider both positive/effective and challenging examples.
- Distinguish observable text from interpretation. Do not infer personality, intent, mental state, or spoken tone from text.
- Account for context: technical disagreement, urgency, role relationships, language fluency, cultural differences, and the other participants' contributions.
- Do not assume that short/direct wording is disrespectful, or that disagreement is a communication failure.
- Do not treat the number of messages as a measure of skill.
- Do not identify or evaluate other people. Focus only on my own observable communication.
- Do not reproduce messages, quote private content, or include names, email addresses, customer/project identifiers, URLs, or other identifying details in the output.
- If a behavior cannot be assessed from written communications, use N/O rather than guessing.
- Avoid false precision. Explain uncertainty and plausible alternative interpretations.

### Required output

Create a report with:

1. **Scope and limitations**
   - Date range and approved source types reviewed.
   - Approximate volume/coverage, if reliably known.
   - Explicit exclusions and known blind spots.
   - Any reason the sample may be biased or incomplete.

2. **Competency scorecard**
   - For each competency: provisional score 1–5 or N/O.
   - Confidence: Low / Medium / High.
   - Brief rationale based only on aggregated, paraphrased patterns.
   - Do not calculate an overall score.

3. **Strengths**
   - Three to five recurring effective behaviors.
   - Why each behavior helps collaboration or delivery.

4. **Development opportunities**
   - Three to five patterns worth experimenting with.
   - Describe behavior, not personality.
   - Explain possible impact without claiming causation where evidence is weak.

5. **Balanced examples**
   - Use abstract, anonymized scenarios only.
   - Never reproduce original message text or identifiable details.

6. **Alternative interpretations**
   - What might be explained by context, role, urgency, missing conversation history, or limitations of text-only evidence?

7. **Self-reflection questions**
   - Questions that help me validate or challenge the analysis.

8. **Practical development experiments**
   - Suggest 3–5 small, observable behaviors to try.
   - Include a situation, behavior, and signal of progress for each.

9. **Confidence and limitations**
   - Clearly state that this is AI-assisted reflection, not an objective or formal performance assessment.

### Final privacy check

Before returning the report, scan your output and remove:
- Names and personal identifiers.
- Verbatim or near-verbatim message excerpts.
- Customer, product, project, or incident identifiers.
- Confidential technical or business details.
- Any content about other people that is not necessary to describe my own behavior.

Return only the sanitized report. Do not create or share a raw-message appendix.
