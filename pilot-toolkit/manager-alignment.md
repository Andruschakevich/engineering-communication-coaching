# Manager Alignment Guide

**Audience:** Participant's manager and program sponsor  
**Purpose:** Align expectations and support without turning coaching into covert performance monitoring.

## Before the pilot

Have a short alignment conversation with the manager/sponsor. Confirm:

1. What business or team need motivated the coaching?
2. What observable behaviors would make the pilot useful?
3. What is within the participant's control?
4. What opportunities can the manager provide for practice?
5. What information may be shared, and what remains private?
6. How will the manager continue normal feedback and accountability?

Avoid vague goals such as “be nicer” or “improve attitude.” Translate concerns into behaviors, for example:
- Express disagreement without personal labels or sarcasm.
- Ask clarifying questions before rebutting.
- Give feedback with specific examples and actionable next steps.
- Structure technical updates around impact, options, and recommendation.
- Facilitate discussions so relevant people can contribute.

## Suggested manager conversation

“The coaching program is intended to help strengthen practical communication behaviors. It is not a substitute for your role as manager, and it is not an investigation. We want to agree on a small number of observable outcomes, provide opportunities to practice, and review progress fairly. The participant should know in advance what information will be shared with you. We will not routinely share private session notes or raw conversation examples.”

## Manager responsibilities during the pilot

- Give timely, specific feedback through normal management channels.
- Recognize positive behavior when it occurs.
- Provide low-risk opportunities to practice facilitation, presentations, and cross-functional communication.
- Be clear about role expectations and consequences through normal processes.
- Avoid asking the coach for confidential details outside the agreement.
- Do not treat a single awkward interaction as proof that coaching failed.

## Progress update format

Use only what was agreed with the participant:

**Period:**  
**Sessions completed:**  
**Agreed development themes:**  
**High-level progress:**  
**Behaviors practiced:**  
**Support or opportunities requested:**  
**Next review point:**  

Avoid:
- Verbatim quotes from private sessions.
- Unnecessary names or incident details.
- Personality labels.
- Unsupported claims that the participant has or has not changed.

## If concerns continue

Coaching is not a substitute for direct management action. If a workplace behavior violates policy or creates a serious risk, the manager should use established company processes. The coach should not investigate or adjudicate allegations.

## End-of-pilot alignment

Review:
- What behaviors have changed, with examples?
- What evidence is still missing?
- What support helped?
- What should continue for 30–90 days?
- What belongs in normal performance management rather than coaching?
