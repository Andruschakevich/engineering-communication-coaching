# Baseline Communication Assessment

**Purpose:** Establish a developmental starting point using multiple perspectives and observable behavioral evidence. This is not a psychometric test, formal performance rating, or standalone employment decision.

## Toolkit files

- **[Baseline assessment workbook (Excel)](./Engineering_communication_pilot_baseline_and_tracking.xlsx)** — scorecard, rating guide, participant goals, manager updates, recording and data agreement, and assessment methodology.
- [Pilot toolkit folder](./) — all files in this toolkit.

Use the workbook alongside this guide. The workbook is the practical assessment and tracking tool; this document explains the methodology and safeguards.

## Assessment perspectives

| Perspective | Owner | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Self-assessment | Participant | Personal perception, reflection, strengths, and development priorities |
| Manager assessment | Manager | Observations from normal work interactions and collaboration |
| AI-assisted assessment | Participant, using an approved AI workflow | Structured review of permitted work communication to identify possible patterns and generate hypotheses for reflection |

**Why “AI-assisted assessment”:** AI can help review a large body of permitted communication consistently, but it can miss context, interpret tone incorrectly, and overgeneralize. Treat its output as a discussion input—not an objective verdict or replacement for human judgment.

## Rating scale

| Score | Behavioral anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Rarely demonstrates the behavior; interactions may be counterproductive |
| 2 | Demonstrates it inconsistently; often needs support |
| 3 | Usually effective in routine situations; less consistent under pressure |
| 4 | Consistently effective, including in challenging situations |
| 5 | A role model who helps others develop the behavior |

Use **N/O (not observed / insufficient evidence)** when a perspective cannot reasonably assess a behavior. Do not force a score.

**Evidence confidence:** Low / Medium / High. Confidence describes the quality and breadth of evidence, not the participant's ability.

## Competency scorecard

The workbook uses the following competencies and observable criteria. Rate each criterion separately from each applicable perspective; use N/O where evidence is insufficient.

| Competency | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| Respectful communication | Respectful wording; professional tone under pressure; respectful humor and boundaries; disagreement without personalization |
| Emotional intelligence | Self-awareness of triggers; self-regulation; awareness of impact |
| Active listening | Attention and turn-taking; clarifying questions; paraphrasing and confirmation |
| Constructive feedback | Specificity and evidence; impact and actionable next step |
| Receiving feedback | Openness and non-defensiveness; accountability and repair |
| Structured communication | Clarity and logical structure; audience adaptation; conciseness and BLUF |
| Influence and conflict | Conflict de-escalation; evidence-based persuasion; decision and action clarity |
| Presentation and facilitation | Presentation structure and delivery; Q&A and handling challenge; meeting facilitation and inclusion |
| Collaboration and ownership | Reliability and follow-through; adaptability and collaboration |

For each criterion, record a short paraphrased example or reflection, evidence confidence, and development priority. Avoid sensitive details and identifying information.

## AI-assisted review of work communication

The participant may use an AI coding assistant (for example, Claude Code) to review work communication from an agreed period, such as the previous six months, **only where company policy and applicable requirements permit this processing**.

### Before starting

- Confirm whether the relevant communication data may be processed by the selected AI provider and tool.
- Confirm the approved account, model, settings, retention, and data-handling requirements with the company.
- Do not assume Claude Code is local-only: prompts and selected context may be sent to the provider.
- Exclude personal conversations, HR/medical/legal matters, credentials, customer secrets, sensitive personal data, and channels or conversations the participant is not authorized to process.
- Do not bypass access controls or collect messages from spaces the participant is not authorized to access.
- Prefer an approved enterprise environment or approved local workflow where available.

**What is shared with the coach/manager:** only the participant-reviewed summary, ratings, confidence, and sanitized behavioral patterns. Do not share raw exports, screenshots, verbatim messages, or third-party identifiers.

### AI review output

For each competency, ask the tool to provide:
- A tentative rating from 1–5 or N/O.
- Confidence (Low / Medium / High) and the amount/type of evidence considered.
- Balanced patterns: effective behaviors as well as possible development areas.
- Short, paraphrased examples with names, project identifiers, and sensitive details removed.
- Alternative interpretations and limitations.
- A distinction between observable communication behavior and inferred intent/personality.

The participant reviews the result, corrects context, and decides what summary is appropriate to share. AI-generated scores remain provisional.

## Evidence prompts

For each competency, ask:
- What behavior is actually observable?
- In what context did it occur?
- What was the likely communication goal?
- What impact is supported by the available evidence?
- Does the pattern recur, or is it based on one isolated event?
- What alternative explanations or missing context may exist?

Include at least one example of effective communication, one challenging interaction, one self-identified strength, and one development priority.

## Participant self-reflection

1. Which communication situations are easiest for me?
2. Which situations are most challenging, and what makes them difficult?
3. What feedback have I heard more than once?
4. What do I want colleagues to experience differently when working with me?
5. What is one small behavior I am willing to practice this week?

## Goal definition

Goals are participant-owned. Make them specific, observable, realistic, and meaningful to the participant.

- **What I want to improve**
- **Why it matters to me**
- **Target behavior (what others could observe)**
- **Situations to practice**
- **Success evidence**
- **Confidence (1–10)**
- **Support needed**
- **Review date**

## Manager / sponsor progress updates

Use the workbook's Manager Updates tab for agreed high-level updates only. Do not include private session notes, verbatim quotes, or sensitive incident details. Record the date, sessions completed, agreed development focus, progress observed (behavior and context), evidence confidence, support/opportunity requested, and next review date.

## Recording, transcription, and data agreement

Complete the workbook's recording and data-handling checklist before recording any session. Recording is optional; declining must not affect participation. Confirm company policy and applicable law. Agree the purpose, access, storage location, transcription/AI provider, retention/deletion, explicit consent of all attendees, and any participant questions or limitations.

## Comparing perspectives

- Do not average the three perspectives or calculate one overall score.
- Discuss meaningful differences and ask what evidence or context explains them.
- A disagreement between self, manager, and AI is a prompt for exploration—not proof that one perspective is wrong.
- Keep private reflections private unless the participant explicitly agrees to share them, subject to organizational policy and applicable requirements.
- Share only the minimum necessary, participant-reviewed summary.

## Reassessment

Repeat the scorecard around the midpoint and at the end. Compare behavior, examples, and confidence—not just numbers. A rating change should be explainable by evidence. Do not use this assessment as a formal employment decision.
