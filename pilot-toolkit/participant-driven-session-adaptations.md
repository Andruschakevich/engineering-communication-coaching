# Participant-Driven Session Adaptations

This guide adapts the reusable eight-session curriculum to development needs raised during the pilot kick-off. It does not replace the core curriculum or create additional sessions. Use the relevant modules below as practical case material, and confirm priorities with the participant as the program progresses.

> Keep this adaptation in the coach's private working context where appropriate. Do not add participant names, raw transcripts, verbatim workplace messages, identifiable incidents, or confidential company information to this reusable repository.

## Needs surfaced during the kick-off

The participant raised the following development themes:

1. **Career progression and promotion readiness:** making contribution visible, understanding expectations for the next level, and communicating evidence of readiness.
2. **Manager communication:** discussing perceived lack of recognition, unclear feedback, and disagreement about decisions or expectations constructively.
3. **Emotional regulation and assertiveness:** expressing a strong point of view without unnecessary escalation, especially in one-to-one conversations.
4. **Professional visibility:** communicating outcomes, initiatives, risks, and contributions to the right audience.
5. **Cross-team influence and escalation:** getting responses and decisions when work is blocked by dependencies or teams outside the participant's direct control.
6. **Proactive updates without micromanagement:** keeping stakeholders informed about status, risks, and dependencies while preserving autonomy.

The participant also explicitly asked for a coaching relationship that includes mentor input: practical examples, alternative approaches, and the coach's perspective—not questions alone.

## Recommended practice cases

Use sanitized versions of situations the participant chooses. Do not assume the participant's interpretation of another person's intention is established fact.

- **Promotion expectations conversation:** ask a manager what specific outcomes and behaviors would demonstrate readiness for the next level; summarize the answer and agree on evidence and a review point.
- **Unclear or disappointing feedback:** clarify the observed behavior, its impact, the expected alternative, and what success would look like.
- **Blocked cross-team dependency:** explain the issue, business or delivery impact, attempts already made, options, and the decision or help required.
- **Status update without micromanagement:** send a short update that distinguishes completed work, current risk, dependency, next action, and when the next update will arrive.
- **Technical contribution visibility:** present an initiative in terms of problem solved, personal contribution, measurable or observable outcome, and learning.
- **Strong disagreement in a one-to-one:** state the concern firmly, support it with evidence, acknowledge the other person's constraints, and make a clear request.

## Coaching delivery: combine coaching and mentoring

The participant said they value dialogue and concrete guidance, including what the coach might do in a similar situation. Preserve participant ownership while making the coach's experience useful.

For relevant exercises, use this sequence:

1. **Explore:** ask the participant to describe the situation, desired outcome, constraints, and what they have tried.
2. **Reflect:** summarize the participant's perspective and check that it is accurate.
3. **Offer a mentor perspective:** with permission, share one relevant experience, principle, or alternative approach. Label it as the coach's perspective, not the only correct answer.
4. **Compare options:** discuss likely benefits, risks, and trade-offs of the participant's approach and the alternatives.
5. **Choose:** let the participant select an approach that fits their context and values.
6. **Rehearse:** practice the actual conversation, message, presentation, or escalation.
7. **Review:** after the real-world attempt, examine what happened and adapt.

Avoid turning the session into advice-giving. The coach should not assume full context, decide who is right in a workplace dispute, or promise a promotion outcome.

## Progress signals

Look for observable changes rather than personality judgments:

- The participant can explain a concern with facts, impact, and a clear request.
- Strong disagreement remains focused on the issue and does not become personal.
- Updates make status, risks, ownership, and next steps clear without unnecessary detail.
- Cross-team requests identify the needed action, owner, and decision date.
- The participant can explain their contribution and impact to different audiences.
- Career-development conversations produce clearer expectations and agreed follow-up.

Revisit these signals during the midpoint review and session 8. Do not treat promotion, manager approval, or another person's response as a direct measure of communication-skill improvement.
