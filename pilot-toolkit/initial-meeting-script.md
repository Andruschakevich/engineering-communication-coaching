# Kick-off & Coaching Agreement — Facilitator Guide

**Suggested duration:** 60 minutes  
**Purpose:** Establish trust, agree on the coaching process and boundaries, understand the participant's goals, and leave with clear next steps.

## Before the meeting

- Prepare the coaching agreement, baseline assessment, and shared schedule.
- Confirm the approved recording/transcription tool and storage location, if recording is permitted.
- Be ready to explain who can access recordings, transcripts, and notes, how long they are retained, and how deletion works.
- Confirm company policy before proposing any AI processing of work communications.
- Do not start recording until every participant has explicitly agreed.

## 60-minute agenda

| Time | Topic | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Recording and transcription consent** | Explain purpose, tool, processing, access, retention, and alternatives; obtain explicit consent before recording |
| 5–10 | Welcome and purpose | Establish a supportive, developmental tone |
| 10–18 | Program overview | Explain the eight-session structure and expected commitment |
| 18–28 | Participant's goals and feedback | Understand the participant's priorities, concerns, and proposals |
| 28–35 | Working agreement and confidentiality | Agree roles, boundaries, manager updates, and data handling |
| 35–42 | Schedule | Agree dates, times, duration, and rescheduling process for all sessions |
| 42–47 | Toastmasters follow-up | Understand progress and identify the next concrete step |
| 47–55 | Baseline assessment | Explain self, manager, and AI-assisted perspectives; agree how each will be completed |
| 55–60 | Recap and homework | Confirm decisions, owners, and deadlines |

## 0. Recording and transcription consent (5 minutes)

Suggested wording:

“Before we get into the agenda, I'd like to ask whether you're comfortable with recording and transcribing our coaching meetings. The purpose would be to help me review the conversation accurately, prepare follow-ups, and avoid missing agreements. I want to be transparent about the tool, where the files are stored, who can access them, and when they will be deleted. Recording and transcription are optional. If you prefer not to record, we'll have the same coaching session and I'll take brief notes instead.”

Before recording, explicitly cover:
- What is recorded: audio, video, transcript, or a combination.
- Which approved tool/provider processes the content, including any AI transcription.
- Whether data is sent to a third party and under what account/settings.
- Storage location, access permissions, retention period, and deletion process.
- Whether excerpts may be used for coaching review (not public examples).
- That consent can be declined or withdrawn; the meeting continues without recording.

**Important:** Ask for consent at the beginning of each meeting, including meetings with additional attendees. Consent to recording does not automatically mean consent to third-party AI transcription or analysis. Follow company policy and applicable law. Do not use an unapproved tool.

## 1. Welcome and purpose (5 minutes)

“I'd like this to be a practical development process. You have valuable technical expertise, and the goal is to make your communication as effective as your engineering contribution. We'll work on real workplace behaviors, practice alternatives, and review what helps. This is not about changing your personality or making you agree with everyone. It's about increasing your ability to communicate clearly, respectfully, and effectively, especially when discussions are difficult.”

Ask:
- “How does that purpose sound to you?”
- “What would make this useful rather than just another meeting?”

## 2. Explain the process (8 minutes)

“The current plan is eight sessions, usually weekly. Each session includes a small amount of theory, practical exercises, roleplay, and one experiment to try at work. We'll review what happened and adjust the plan based on evidence.”

The eight sessions:
1. Self-awareness & communication assessment
2. Respectful communication & emotional intelligence
3. Active listening & constructive feedback
4. Structured communication
5. Public speaking & presentations
6. Conflict resolution & influencing
7. Leadership communication & professional presence
8. Review & 90-day development plan

The participant's goals take priority over a rigid agenda. The coach may challenge behavior directly but respectfully. The participant owns their goals and practice between sessions. This is development coaching, not a performance hearing.

## 3. Explore the participant's perspective (10 minutes)

**Strengths**
- “What communication situations do you handle well?”
- “What do colleagues value about working with you?”
- “When have you helped a technical discussion move forward?”

**Challenges**
- “Which conversations tend to become difficult?”
- “What feedback about your communication have you heard more than once?”
- “What do you find frustrating about the way people communicate at work?”
- “When do you notice yourself becoming impatient, sarcastic, or defensive, if at all?”

**Desired outcome**
- “What would you like to do more effectively?”
- “What would a teammate notice if this coaching worked?”
- “Which one change would have the greatest positive impact?”

If the participant disagrees with feedback, do not force agreement. Explore evidence and alternative explanations.

## 4. Working agreement and boundaries (7 minutes)

Agree explicitly:
- Voluntary participation and the ability to raise concerns.
- Respectful candor in both directions.
- What is private and what high-level progress information may be shared with the manager.
- How examples, notes, recordings, and transcripts are handled.
- Limits of confidentiality under policy and applicable law.
- Rescheduling and pause process.

Private reflections and detailed session notes are not routinely shared with the manager. Agree on any high-level updates in advance.

## 5. Schedule (7 minutes)

Agree all eight meeting dates/times, duration, channel, and rescheduling expectations. Put the schedule in a shared calendar or agreed document.

## 6. Toastmasters follow-up (5 minutes)

The prior action was to explore and arrange attendance at a first Toastmasters meeting. Do not assume the participant has already attended. Ask:
- “Did you get a chance to look for a club?”
- “Have you found a meeting that could work?”
- “What is the next realistic step—contacting the club, registering, or attending?”

The expected outcome today is a clear next action or plan, not necessarily completed attendance.

## 7. Baseline assessment (8 minutes)

Explain the three perspectives:
- **Self-assessment:** the participant's own reflection and rating.
- **Manager assessment:** manager's observations from normal work interactions.
- **AI-assisted assessment:** the participant's review of permitted work communication using a company-approved AI workflow.

AI-assisted assessment is a source of hypotheses, not an objective verdict. The raw messages are not shared with the coach or manager. Only a participant-reviewed summary, ratings, confidence, and sanitized patterns may be shared. Confirm company approval before processing work messages with Claude Code or any external AI provider. Claude Code is not automatically local-only.

Keep the participant self-reflection exercise. Ask the participant to identify strengths, challenges, and 3–5 personal goals.

## 8. Close and homework (5 minutes)

Confirm the participant's priorities, one strength to build on, all session dates, recording/data agreements, Toastmasters next step, homework owners, and deadlines.

“Thank you for being open. We do not need to solve everything at once. Let's test one small change, look at what happens, and learn from it.”

## Homework

### 1. Toastmasters
Find a suitable club, check its schedule, contact/register if needed, and choose a first meeting to attend. Bring back the next step or any obstacle.

### 2. AI-assisted communication review
Review work communications from the agreed six-month period only if company policy permits the specific data and AI tool. Use an approved enterprise environment or approved local workflow where available. Do not assume Claude Code is local-only. Do not bypass access controls or include personal, customer, HR, legal, credential, or otherwise sensitive material.

Do not share raw messages, exports, screenshots, names, or project identifiers with the coach or manager. Bring only:
- Provisional 1–5 / N/O ratings for relevant competencies.
- Confidence (Low / Medium / High).
- Balanced strengths and possible development patterns.
- Short, paraphrased and sanitized examples.
- Limitations, alternative interpretations, and context the AI may have missed.
- the participant's own agreement/disagreement with the findings.

### 3. Self-reflection and personal goals
Define 3–5 goals:
- What do I want to improve or strengthen?
- Why does it matter?
- What behavior do I want to change or demonstrate?
- In which situations will I practice?
- How will I measure progress?
- What support do I need?

### 4. Baseline completion
the participant completes the self-assessment. The manager completes their own column independently. Discuss differences; do not average scores.

## Facilitator reminders

- Ask permission before discussing a sensitive example.
- Avoid labels such as “rude,” “toxic,” “difficult,” or “immature.” Describe actions and impact.
- Do not request raw exports to be shared with the coach.
- Do not treat refusal to record or use AI as lack of engagement.
- Do not share participant-specific notes in the reusable program repository.
